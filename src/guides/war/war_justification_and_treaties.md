# War Justification and Treaties

## War System – Staff Responsibilities

The war system requires active Admin oversight. No war may begin without Admin approval. Staff affiliated with any participating nation must not oversee that conflict.

## War Declaration Approval

- All war declarations must be submitted to an Admin or war manager via a ticket before any hostilities begin.

- The reviewing Admin must confirm that a valid Casus Belli applies, that required prior diplomatic attempts are documented (for a War of Annexation), and that neither they nor any available staff have a conflict of interest.

- If all Admins are affiliated with a participating nation, the war declaration is suspended until a non-affiliated Admin is available to review it.

- Approvals must be logged with the nations involved, Casus Belli type, approving Admin, and date.

- If you feel an Admin is biased, you can appeal. If appeals are used repeatedly, the war will be annulled, and the side that appealed can no longer declare war.

- Diplomacy involves asking for provinces or issuing an ultimatum. It must involve roleplay, rather than just “I want this. Now give it to me.” Roleplay is crucial for diplomacy to qualify as justification for a war declaration.

## Casus Belli Reference

| War type | Requirement | Key limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conquest | All diplomatic attempts to acquire a town or territory have failed (dated screenshots required).<br>A reason for the territorial claim is required.<br>For example: a historical, regional, or strategic claim; reclaiming lost land; or another valid reason to claim the land. | The only type allowing conquest. Prior diplomacy and a reason for the claim are required; otherwise, it is warmongering. |
| Despoliation | Seeking to take non-territorial valuables.<br>Raids, player killings, and theft can be used as justification (dated screenshots required). | Cannot occupy or claim land.<br>Requires less justification and allows smaller demands for money or loot than the Economic conflict Casus Belli. |
| Rebellion | Overthrowing an occupying or annexing force. | Failure means the nation cannot rebel again.<br>Both sides can call in allies. |
| Subjugation | Imposing your will on a nation by force.<br>Diplomatic attempts to achieve the goal have failed (dated screenshots required). | Cannot be indefinite. The target must still be able to defend itself. |
| Humiliation | The target leader has been repeatedly hostile to the attacker (dated screenshots required).<br>An insulting monument has been built (screenshot of the monument required).<br>Diplomatic insults (screenshot of the chat required). | The target must have been extremely hostile. |

| Retaliation | Undoing the effects of subjugation or recovering losses from a Despoliation or Economic conflict war (screenshot of the treaty required).<br>Raids or citizens killed by the defending nation (dated screenshots required). | Only after a truce period when undoing subjugation or recovering losses from Despoliation or Economic conflict wars.<br>Cannot undo another war of retaliation. |

## Treaties

- Violating treaties is not against the rules. Admins will not enforce treaty terms beyond truce periods. The violation of a treaty can be used as a Casus Belli against another nation.

- Treaties must not destroy a nation. Demands such as 100 million ducats a week are therefore not allowed. Demands must follow the treaty rules for the relevant Casus Belli listed below.

## Ally Restrictions

Ally limits are enforced automatically through war sickness, rather than by Admins. War sickness is applied dynamically based on the number of allied nations on each side in a siege zone. If a side exceeds its limit, the allied nations that entered most recently receive war sickness until a slot becomes available.

| War type | Ally restriction |
| --- | --- |
| Conquest | 4 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Despoliation | 1 for attackers and 2 for defenders (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Rebellion | 2 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Subjugation | 3 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Humiliation | No allies allowed |
| Liberation | 2 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Retaliation | 2 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Secession | 3 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Economic conflict | 1 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Treaty violation | 2 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Coalition war | 9 per side (vassals and protectorates included) |
| Holy war | 4 per side of the same religion (vassals and protectorates included) |

## Truces

- Truces are always enforced by Admins.

- The truce duration is determined by the war Admin who approved the war.

- Truces can be appealed if they are too long or too short. A high-ranking Admin will then review the truce.

- Truces do not prevent raids or other acts of aggression.

- Treaty violation wars are the only wars that can ignore truces.

- The minimum truce duration is always 1 siege session.

| War type | Truces |
| --- | --- |
| Conquest | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Despoliation | Maximum 2 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Rebellion | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Subjugation | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Humiliation | Maximum 4 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Liberation | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Retaliation | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Secession | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Economic conflict | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Treaty violation | Maximum 2 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Coalition war | Maximum 4 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |
| Holy war | Maximum 3 siege sessions depending on the severity of the treaty |

## Declaration of a Coalition War

To declare a coalition war, the accusing side must meet all three requirements below.

- The coalition must have at least **two** of the following reasons for its war to be approved:

| Reason | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Conquering strength | The accused party has won **three** or more attacking sieges and conquered **three** towns within **five** siege sessions, whether the towns belong to the same nation or different nations. **Screenshots are required.** |
| Menacing military strength | The accused party has exceptional military strength, demonstrated by the number of players who show up for wars. **Screenshots are required.** |
| Betrayal or treachery | The accused party has recently betrayed or violated a pact, treaty, or agreement with one or more nations in the coalition. **Screenshots are required.** |
| Humiliation and insults | The accused party has recently humiliated or publicly insulted at least **two** nations in the coalition. **Screenshots are required.** |

- Once the reasons for the coalition war are approved, the accusing party **must** hold a roleplay meeting to establish the temporary coalition. All coalition members must attend. The meeting must be recorded, and the clip must be sent to staff in the war ticket for review. If staff find the meeting inappropriate, they will deny the coalition, and another meeting must take place. After the **third** denial, the coalition war can no longer be approved.

- Coalitions can last for a maximum of **4 siege sessions**, followed by a cooldown of **1 siege session**. This allows diplomacy to develop between the accused party and the accusing nations. During the cooldown, **no one may declare another coalition war** against the previously accused party.
